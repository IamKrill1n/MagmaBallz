# Equational implication over magmas: strategies and theorems

Research date: 2026-08-15

## Conclusion

The benchmark asks a classical **equational consequence** question.  For magma
terms `s`, `t`, `u`, and `v`, it asks whether

\[
  (\forall \bar x,\ s(\bar x)=t(\bar x))
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  (\forall \bar y,\ u(\bar y)=v(\bar y))
\]

holds in every magma.  Birkhoff completeness says that a true implication has
a finite equational derivation using substitution, equality, and congruence.
Thus proof enumeration is a complete semi-decision procedure for the `true`
side.  A false implication has a model of the premise in which one assignment
falsifies the conclusion, but that model need not be finite.  Consequently,
finite-table search is only a semi-decision procedure for the subset of false
instances with finite countermodels.

There can be no total algorithm for arbitrary input identities in this
signature.  McKenzie proved that, in a language with just one binary operation,
it is undecidable whether a single identity has a nontrivial model, and also
whether it has a finite nontrivial model.  Fixing the target identity to the
singleton law `x = y` reduces either problem to implication: `E => (x = y)`
holds exactly when `E` has no model with two distinct elements.  This does not
make the competition's fixed, finite corpus undecidable; it rules out a general
complete solver and explains why a resource-bounded portfolio is the right
architecture.  [McKenzie, 1975, publisher's article and extract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-symbolic-logic/article/on-spectra-and-the-negative-solution-of-the-decision-problem-for-identities-having-a-finite-nontrivial-model1/101AF2F2F2858A6265074FD677B154E0)

For this benchmark, the strongest practical design is therefore a fair,
deterministic portfolio:

1. canonicalize and try closed-form implications;
2. interleave reconstructible equational proof search with exhaustive size-2
   and size-3 countermodel search;
3. deepen the proof side through short substitution chains, bidirectional
   rewriting, equality saturation, and superposition;
4. deepen the model side through structured finite families and a fixed-size
   constraint solver/backtracker, increasing the size only as budget permits;
5. validate every proposed proof or table independently and emit the smallest
   Lean certificate found.

The sections below separate sourced mathematical facts from recommendations.
Statements prefixed **Inference** are engineering conclusions drawn from the
sources rather than theorems stated by them.  Statements prefixed **Local
evidence** report this repository's prototypes and are not mathematical or
held-back-benchmark guarantees.

## Precise framing and certificate contract

A magma is a carrier `G` with a binary operation `G -> G -> G`.  A magma term is
a full binary tree whose leaves are variables.  A law is a pair of terms and is
satisfied when the two evaluations agree for every assignment of its variables.
The official Equational Theories Project (ETP) uses this same definition and
writes `E |= E'` when every magma satisfying `E` satisfies `E'`.
[ETP paper, Sections 1.1 and 2](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=2)

For a positive answer, this repository asks Lean for

```text
forall (G : Type) [Magma G], EquationLHS G -> EquationRHS G
```

For a negative answer the generated Lean goal asks for

```text
∃ (G : Type) (_ : Magma G), EquationLHS G ∧ ¬ EquationRHS G
```

The README's stated competition contract expects a finite magma witness, and
the canonical certificate is a complete operation table on `Fin n`, checked by
Lean.  The proposition generated by the current judge does not itself include a
finiteness hypothesis, however: a constructively defined infinite countermodel
could inhabit the displayed goal if it can be expressed using allowed
dependencies.  See the local [README certificate forms](../../../README.md#answer-format),
[judge goal construction](../../../judge/verify.py), and the judge-provided
helpers [DecideBang.lean](../../../judge/JudgeDecide/DecideBang.lean) and
[MemoFinOp.lean](../../../judge/JudgeFinOp/MemoFinOp.lean).

Two scope points matter:

- The semantic question in the positive branch ranges over arbitrary magmas,
  not merely finite ones.
- The README's stated negative-certificate contract is narrower than semantic
  non-implication: it demands a **finite** witness.  Some genuine
  non-implications have no such witness.  Any corpus intended to be total under
  that stated two-certificate contract must therefore select false cases for
  which a finite certificate exists; a solver cannot manufacture a finite one
  for an Austin pair.  This is a contract-level limitation, not a restriction
  expressed in the current Lean `Goal` type.

The usual universal-algebra convention takes carriers to be nonempty.  Lean's
`Type` also admits the empty type, but this does not affect the undecidability
reduction above: `x = y` is vacuously true on an empty carrier and true on a
singleton, while any carrier with two distinct elements falsifies it.

## Mathematical foundations that directly guide search

### Birkhoff completeness: semantic consequence equals finite derivability

For a theory `Gamma`, the ETP formalization defines derivability using premise,
reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, substitution of arbitrary terms for
variables, and compatibility with the magma operation.  Its formalized
Birkhoff completeness theorem states

\[
  \Gamma \vdash s \simeq t \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \Gamma \models s \simeq t.
\]

[ETP blueprint, Definition 1.7 and Theorem 1.8](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/basic-theory-chapter.html#theorem-18)
This is the magma instance of Birkhoff's foundational result.
[Birkhoff, *On the Structure of Abstract Algebras*, 1935](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematical-proceedings-of-the-cambridge-philosophical-society/article/on-the-structure-of-abstract-algebras/D142B3886A3B7A218D8DF8E6DDA2B5B1)

Consequences for a solver:

- Enumerating finite derivation trees is complete for true implications, but
  there is no computable global bound sufficient to turn this enumeration into
  a decision procedure for unrestricted inputs.
- The natural proof IR needs only `refl`, an instantiated hypothesis, `symm`,
  `trans`, and congruence under the binary operation.  This is also exactly the
  fragment from which MagmaEgg reconstructed Lean proofs in the ETP.
  [ETP paper, proof reconstruction](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=42)
- A failed bounded proof search says nothing about validity.

### Free magma modulo the premise: one canonical model decides the question

Let `X` contain the variables of the goal and let `M_(X,E1)` be the free magma
on `X` subject to `E1`, constructed by quotienting raw terms by the congruence
generated by `E1`.  Then `E1 |= (u = v)` exactly when the equivalence classes of
`u` and `v` are equal in this free magma.  If they differ, the free magma itself
is a countermodel.  [ETP blueprint, Theorems 10.2--10.3 and Corollary
10.4](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/abstract-nonsense-chapter.html)

This is conceptually decisive but not automatically computational: the quotient
word problem can be undecidable and the free model is generally infinite.
When `E1` admits a terminating confluent presentation or a computable canonizer,
however, canonical forms turn the semantic criterion into an algorithm.  The
ETP used canonizers as computable invariants for refutations.
[ETP paper, Section 6.3](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=35)

### HSP closure and reusable model families

Birkhoff's variety theorem identifies equational classes as exactly the classes
closed under homomorphic images, subalgebras, and products.  In particular,
`Mod(E1)` is a variety and `E1 |= E2` means that `E2` is an identity throughout
that variety.  [Birkhoff, 1935](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100013463)

Practical uses are modest but real:

- a submagma of a premise-model remains a premise-model;
- a direct product of premise-models remains a premise-model;
- a homomorphic image remains a premise-model, although quotienting can destroy
  the particular disequality witnessing failure of `E2`;
- a smallest closed submagma containing a falsifying assignment is therefore a
  sound way to shrink a countermodel before rendering it.

The last point is an **inference** from closure under subalgebras, not a guarantee
that every found table has a proper shrink.

### Cheap theorems and invariants

The formal ETP development supplies several low-cost transformations:

- **Duality.** Reversing every operation preserves implication:
  `E1 |= E2` iff `dual(E1) |= dual(E2)`.
  [ETP blueprint, Lemma 3.5](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/general-implications-chapter.html)
- **Diagonalization.** If the variables on the two sides of `F(xbar) = G(ybar)`
  are disjoint, two instances eliminate `G` and show that `F` is constant over
  its arguments; this often collapses a law to the singleton or constant class.
  [ETP blueprint, Theorem 3.6](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/general-implications-chapter.html)
- **Variable multiplicity.** If both sides of `E1` contain each variable equally
  often, every consequence must have the same property.  A multiset-addition
  magma proves the obstruction.
  [ETP blueprint, Theorem 3.8](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/general-implications-chapter.html)
- **Other matching invariants.** Leftmost leaf, rightmost leaf, ordered leaf
  sequence, multiplicities modulo `m`, and computable images in suitable free
  objects can rule out implications.
  [ETP paper, Section 6.2](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=34)

These are both triage features and certificate recipes.  An invariant mismatch
is not itself a finite witness, but many basic invariants have small finite
realizations: projections realize leftmost/rightmost-leaf invariants, and finite
cyclic linear magmas often realize multiplicity obstructions.

### Finiteness creates extra implications

On finite sets, injective self-maps are surjective, right inverses are left
inverses, and iterates are eventually periodic.  The ETP formalizes these facts
and uses them to prove implications valid only for finite magmas.
[ETP blueprint, Lemmas 5.5--5.7](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/infinite-model-chapter.html)

An **Austin law** has an infinite nontrivial model but no finite nontrivial
model.  More generally, an Austin pair `(E1,E2)` satisfies `E1 |=fin E2` but not
`E1 |= E2`.  The ETP records 820 such implications among its order-at-most-four
laws and gives explicit infinite countermodels.
[ETP blueprint, Chapter 5](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/infinite-model-chapter.html)
Therefore neither exhaustive finite search nor an `unsat` result at every
tested size proves unrestricted implication.

## Decidability and semi-decidability boundaries

| Problem | Status | Reason and caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Unrestricted `E1 |= E2` for arbitrary input magma identities | Undecidable | Reduce McKenzie's undecidable nontrivial-model problem to `E1 |= (x=y)`. |
| `E1 |=fin E2` over all finite magmas | Undecidable | The same reduction uses McKenzie's finite-nontrivial-model result. |
| True unrestricted implications | Recursively enumerable | Enumerate Birkhoff derivations; completeness guarantees eventual success. |
| False implications having a finite countermodel | Recursively enumerable | Enumerate sizes, all operation tables, and goal assignments. |
| A fixed carrier size `n` | Decidable | There are finitely many tables and assignments; SAT/CSP is an equivalent finite encoding. |
| A fixed finite corpus such as the competition files | Decidable in principle | It can be tabulated once; this says nothing about generalization to unseen larger laws. |

The first two rows are directly verified by McKenzie's theorem and the stated
reduction.  The next three are elementary consequences of Birkhoff completeness
and finite enumeration.  The final row is a finite-set observation.

For a carrier of size `n` there are exactly `n^(n^2)` labelled binary operation
tables.  Thus sizes 2 and 3 contain only 16 and 19,683 tables, while size 4
contains 4,294,967,296.  This calculation is an **inference** directly from the
definition of a table.  The ETP exhaustively searched through size 4, used only
524 distinct witnesses to refute 96.3% of all false order-at-most-four
implications, and reported 165 CPU-hours for that campaign.
[ETP paper, Section 5.1](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=20)

## Existing proof-search methods

### 1. Closed-form and bounded substitution synthesis

Try, in increasing proof cost:

- reflexivity and premise/goal equality up to side swap and variable relabeling;
- a single total substitution instance of either orientation of `E1`;
- one instance under a subterm context (one or more congruence wrappers);
- two applications joined by transitivity, solving the shared midpoint by
  unification rather than merely comparing partially instantiated terms;
- diagonalization, derived constancy, and singleton collapse;
- the dual problem when its canonical form is cheaper or already supported.

These are instances of Birkhoff's rules, so every emitted proof is sound after
endpoint checking.  They are attractive because the search space can be
explicitly stratified by term size and proof cost.  The ETP found about 15,000
direct implications by brute-force simple rewriting; transitive closure exposed
about four million more relations in its fixed graph.
[ETP paper, Section 6.1](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=33)

**Local evidence.** The repository has already selected closed-form synthesis,
short-chain unification, specialized algebraic synthesis, and bidirectional
subterm rewriting as its four proof lanes.  It also specifies a reconstructible
derivation DAG over `refl`, `hyp`, `symm`, `trans`, and `congr`.
[Local proof-portfolio decision](../issues/03-choose-proof-search-portfolio.md)
The recorded prototype was a shape test, not broad coverage evidence.

### 2. Bidirectional rewrite search and iterative deepening

Treat an edge as replacing a matched subterm with the opposite side of a fully
instantiated `E1`, in either direction.  Search outward from both goal endpoints
and stop on a collision.  Useful controls are:

- iterative deepening on proof length, intermediate-term size, and substitution
  pool size;
- canonical variable naming and hash-consed terms;
- stable subterm-position and substitution order;
- parent pointers carrying the exact instantiated premise and context;
- subsumption by the cheapest proof of the same term.

Unbounded, fair enumeration is complete by Birkhoff; every practical bound is
incomplete.  Meet-in-the-middle search reduces depth at the cost of storing both
frontiers.  The ETP's equation-search tool similarly searched outward from
hypotheses and goals using substitutions and an implication graph.
[ETP blueprint, Equation Search](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/equation-search-chapter.html)

For this repository, graph-derived lemmas belong to the optional oracle
subsystem when they depend on known equation IDs.  The same mechanism becomes
ordinary reasoning only when the intermediate identities are derived from the
current premise during the case run.

### 3. Term rewriting and Knuth--Bendix completion

Orient `E1` by a well-founded reduction order, simplify terms, generate critical
pairs, and add oriented consequences until the system is convergent.  If a
terminating confluent presentation is obtained, equality is decided by comparing
normal forms.  Completion may fail to orient an equation, generate an unbounded
set of rules, or run forever; those outcomes do not decide the original problem.
The original algorithm was introduced for word problems in universal algebras
with exactly this success-without-general-termination character.
[Knuth and Bendix, *Simple Word Problems in Universal Algebras*, 1970](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/cs257/archive/don-knuth/knuth-bendix.pdf)

This lane is especially promising for collapsing or size-decreasing premises.
It is less suitable when either side introduces variables absent from the other:
such a law cannot simply be applied as a deterministic rewrite without choosing
values for the extra variables.

### 4. Equality saturation with e-graphs

An e-graph compactly stores many terms and their congruence classes, allowing
both orientations to be explored without choosing a single global normal form.
Equality saturation repeatedly matches and applies the premise until the goal
endpoints merge or resource bounds are hit.  Proof explanations must retain
substitutions and congruence edges so they can be reconstructed in Lean.

The ETP used `lean-egg` and a magma-specific `MagmaEgg`.  It explicitly handled
extra variables by instantiating them with terms found in the proof context or
goal subexpressions, and reconstructed explanations into Lean proof terms.
[ETP blueprint, E-Graphs](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/egraphs-chapter.html)
The underlying `egg` implementation describes e-graphs as compact congruence
representations and equality saturation as their rewrite-driven application.
[Willsey et al., *egg: Fast and Extensible E-graphs*, 2021](https://www.mwillsey.com/papers/egg)

Ground congruence closure alone is not enough: the premise is universally
quantified.  Non-ground search needs a fair instantiation policy such as
e-matching, which remains a semi-decision procedure here.
[ETP paper, Section 7.1.2](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=40)

### 5. Superposition/saturation ATPs

Encode `E1` as a universally quantified equality, negate `E2` using fresh
witness constants, clausify, and run a prover such as Vampire or Prover9.
Superposition is designed for first-order equality and can discover lemmas whose
intermediate terms are hard for local rewrite search to guess.  The ETP used
both tools because their solved sets were complementary, and translated proof
steps back into Lean rather than trusting a yes/no result.
[ETP paper, Sections 7.1--7.3](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=39)
The project specifically reports 1,775 implications found with Vampire and a
Lean elaborator for the superposition steps.
[ETP blueprint, Vampire](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/vampire-chapter.html)

**Inference.** In the competition's single-file, sandboxed setting, an external
ATP is useful only if its binary is available or its essential search is
reimplemented.  The lasting design lesson is to separate discovery from trusted
certificate reconstruction.

### 6. Specialized algebraic reasoning

Rewrite laws in terms of left/right translations `L_x`, `R_x`, and the squaring
map `S`.  Equations such as `L_a R_b = id` expose injectivity, surjectivity,
cancellation, or inverse relations that a syntactic term search may rediscover
only after long chains.  For unrestricted implications these arguments must not
use finiteness; for finite-only reasoning, injective/surjective equivalence and
eventual periodicity become available.  The ETP's difficult finite searches and
proofs repeatedly used exactly these derived map properties.
[ETP paper, Sections 2, 7.3.8, and 8](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=50)

## Existing countermodel and model-finding methods

### 1. Exhaustive tiny tables and shared model signatures

For each table, evaluate `E1` on every assignment; only premise-models need goal
testing.  A countermodel is a premise-model with at least one goal-falsifying
assignment.  Across many cases, compute once the bitset of corpus laws satisfied
by each table.  One table then refutes every pair `(E1,E2)` for which the first
bit is set and the second is clear.  This signature reuse is how a small witness
set can cover millions of implication pairs, as the ETP results above show.

**Local evidence.** The repository's deterministic prototype found 6 of 145
development false cases first at size 2 and 34 more at size 3.  Structured
families and seeded size-4 backtracking raised the total to 99, with no benefit
from 46,000 pseudo-random candidates on the residue.
[Local countermodel-portfolio decision](../issues/02-choose-countermodel-search-portfolio.md)
These figures are public-corpus engineering evidence, not a theorem about
order-five generalization.

### 2. Fixed-size SAT/SMT/CSP encoding

For a chosen `n`, introduce one finite-domain variable `op[i,j]` for every table
cell.  For every assignment of the premise variables, introduce or inline term
evaluation and assert equality of the two premise terms.  Introduce one
assignment of the goal variables and assert disequality of its two term values.
The resulting constraint problem is satisfiable exactly when an `n`-element
countermodel exists.

Useful implementation techniques are:

- increase `n` incrementally;
- flatten nested terms with auxiliary definitions;
- reuse clauses learned at the same size where the encoding permits it;
- add sound carrier-permutation symmetry breaking;
- branch on table cells with equality propagation;
- extract a total table and recheck it independently.

Claessen and Soerensson's Paradox work describes this MACE-style pipeline and
the specific improvements of term definitions, incremental SAT, static symmetry
reduction, and sort inference.
[Primary publication record, Chalmers](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/2547)

Z3 can also find finite models of quantified formulas, and the official ETP
script encodes the operation as an uninterpreted binary function, negates the
implication, asks Z3 for a model, and emits Lean source from the resulting
table. [ETP `generate_z3_counterexample.py`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teorth/equational_theories/main/scripts/generate_z3_counterexample.py)
However, Z3's own guide stresses that quantified reasoning is incomplete in
general and may diverge.  An explicit fixed finite sort or bit-vector encoding
turns the bounded problem into a genuine decision problem.
[Official Z3 quantifier guide](https://microsoft.github.io/z3guide/docs/logic/Quantifiers/)

### 3. Direct finite model builders

Mace4 builds all ground instances at a selected domain size and uses a ground
equational-rewriting decision procedure to search for a table.  Its author
describes it as complementary to a theorem prover and particularly useful for
finite algebras and equational problems.
[McCune, *Mace4 Reference Manual and Guide*](https://www.cs.unm.edu/~mccune/mace4/mace4.pdf)
The ETP found well-configured Mace4 substantially faster than a generic
SAT-based finite model builder on some project equations, while Vampire's
portfolio mode sometimes found an unspecified-size model faster.  Those are
project observations, not universal benchmarks.
[ETP paper, Section 7.3.1](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=44)

### 4. Constraint-propagating partial-table backtracking

Assign operation cells in a fixed order.  Whenever all cells needed to evaluate
a premise instance are known, reject the branch if that instance fails.  Seed a
goal-falsifying assignment early, and propagate equalities between partially
evaluated terms.  Retained premise-models can order cell values without deleting
branches.  This is a direct, deterministic analogue of SEM/Mace-style table
construction and naturally supports pause/resume by storing its stack.

**Inference.** For these very small signatures, a specialized evaluator can
avoid the generic clausification overhead of an ATP.  It should nevertheless
borrow model-finder techniques: least-number/symmetry rules, most-constrained
cell selection when deterministic, and aggressive equality propagation.

### 5. Structured finite families

Cheap, incomplete families have high value before generic size-4+ search:

- constant and left/right projection operations;
- min/max, bands, semilattices, groups, and quasigroups;
- affine or linear operations `x diamond y = a*x + b*y + c` over `Z/nZ` or a
  finite field;
- small quadratic families;
- products of smaller examples;
- projection tables with a small number of exceptional cells;
- translation-invariant operations on a finite group.

The ETP reports linear and quadratic magmas, cancellative magmas, quasigroups,
translation-invariant constructions, and hand-guided finite models as productive
sources. [ETP blueprint, Selected magmas](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/selected-magmas-chapter.html)
For a linear magma, every term evaluates to a linear combination whose
coefficients are polynomials in `a` and `b`; implication obstructions can
therefore be searched algebraically, including with Groebner bases in the
commutative case. [ETP paper, Section 5.2](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=22)

The structured lane should enumerate parameters deterministically and dedupe
tables before universal premise checking.  Random unrestricted tables are weak
because they tend to satisfy few nontrivial laws; the ETP made the same empirical
observation at size 5.
[ETP paper, Section 5.1](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=21)

### 6. Infinite models and why they still matter here

An unrestricted non-implication may require a free-algebra, string, arithmetic,
translation-invariant, or greedy infinite construction.  The ETP developed
translation-invariant magmas of the form

\[
  x \diamond y = x + f(y-x)
\]

and greedy extensions of partial operations to resolve hard anti-implications.
[ETP blueprint, Infinite magma constructions](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/infinite-magma-constructions-chapter.html)

These methods explain residual cases and can refute a proposed global theorem,
but they do not meet this benchmark's false-certificate contract unless the
construction can be specialized to a finite model.  In particular, lack of a
finite witness should not cause the proof lane to assert `true`; it may indicate
an Austin pair or simply that the smallest finite witness exceeds the budget.

### 7. Countermodel minimization and canonicalization

After a table is found:

1. keep a concrete assignment falsifying `E2`;
2. compute the submagma generated by the values in that assignment;
3. if it is proper, restrict the table and recheck both laws exhaustively;
4. relabel elements to a canonical deterministic order;
5. emit the table only after shape, range, premise, and failure checks pass.

This can reduce Lean source size and exhaustive checking cost.  Do not quotient
by a congruence unless the falsifying values remain distinct after quotienting.

## Recommended benchmark portfolio

The following is an engineering recommendation synthesized from the cited
results and the repository's prototype decisions.

### Phase A: canonicalization and cheap classification

- Parse to an immutable tree representation and alpha-normalize variables by
  first occurrence.
- Canonicalize equality side order for fingerprints while retaining the required
  output orientation.
- Compute dual, variable sets, multiplicities, leaf sequence, leftmost/rightmost
  leaves, subterms, and sizes.
- Discharge reflexive, direct-instance, disjoint-variable diagonalization, and
  singleton/constant-collapse patterns.
- Probe the shared size-2 and size-3 table bank immediately.

This phase should be exhaustive over its declared finite spaces and produce an
exact derivation or table, never a verdict score.

### Phase B: balanced proof and countermodel deepening

Interleave deterministic effort slices rather than choosing one direction
irrevocably:

| Proof lane | Countermodel lane |
|---|---|
| direct premise substitutions | exhaustive size 2, then size 3 |
| one-context and two-hop chains | structured families, sizes 4--7 |
| specialized constancy/map lemmas | constraint backtracking at size 4 |
| bidirectional rewrite frontiers | fixed-size SAT/CSP or Mace-style search |
| equality saturation | increasing sizes while budget remains |
| superposition/ATP fallback | selected larger structured/seeded searches |

The direction predictor, if enabled, may decide which lane gets the first slice
but is not evidence.  A surviving premise-model is reusable ordering information
and may become a countermodel later; it is not yet a candidate.  This follows
the repository glossary's distinction between a premise-model and a
countermodel.

### Phase C: corpus-level reuse without confusing it with reasoning

For Marathon runs, share:

- parsed canonical terms and substitution pools;
- each finite magma's satisfied-law signature;
- derived identities with reconstructible proofs;
- canonical tables and failed exact candidate fingerprints.

Known pair-specific certificates and an implication graph are a **certificate
cache** in this repository's terminology.  They can be valuable in oracle mode
but do not establish generalization.  Content fingerprints, not equation-list
positions, must bind cache entries; IDs are enumeration metadata, not implication
topology. [Existing local research on equation order](equation-order-topology.md)

### Phase D: certificate-first admission

Before paying for a judge call:

- recompute every derivation node's endpoints and require the root to match the
  goal exactly;
- require substitutions to be total for every premise variable used;
- render shared proof DAG nodes once as `have` bindings;
- exhaustively verify table dimensions, values, every premise assignment, and a
  concrete failing goal assignment;
- canonicalize and deduplicate evidence;
- leave enough deadline for Lean compilation.

The search program is an untrusted discovery engine.  Acceptance comes only
from the local checks plus the deterministic Lean judge, matching the ETP's
successful pattern of translating ATP/model output into kernel-checked proof
objects. [ETP paper, Sections 3 and 7.2](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf#page=10)

## Claims a solver must not make

- `No model of size <= n` does **not** imply `E1 |= E2`.
- `Z3 returned unknown`, ATP timeout, e-graph saturation limit, or empty bounded
  rewrite frontier is not mathematical evidence either way.
- A model satisfying `E1` is only a premise-model until a specific assignment
  falsifies `E2`.
- A syntactic invariant mismatch proves non-implication only when the invariant
  is justified by a model/free-object argument; it is not enough that a feature
  merely correlates with labels.
- An implication valid on all tested finite models need not hold on arbitrary
  magmas; Austin pairs are explicit counterexamples.
- An ATP proof or model is not a benchmark answer until it has been translated
  into and accepted as the required Lean certificate.
- The complete order-at-most-four ETP graph is a finite solved artifact.  Its
  impressive coverage statistics should guide strategy order, but they do not
  prove performance on the roughly 62,000 order-five laws used by Stage 2.

## Source quality and limitations

The central sources are primary:

- the [ETP contributors' paper](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/paper.pdf),
  [formal blueprint](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/blueprint/),
  and [first-party repository](https://github.com/teorth/equational_theories);
- Birkhoff's [original 1935 paper](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100013463);
- McKenzie's [original 1975 paper](https://doi.org/10.2307/2271899);
- Knuth and Bendix's [original completion paper](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/cs257/archive/don-knuth/knuth-bendix.pdf);
- the authors' or institutions' first-party documentation for
  [Mace4](https://www.cs.unm.edu/~mccune/mace4/mace4.pdf),
  [Paradox](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/2547),
  [`egg`](https://www.mwillsey.com/papers/egg), and
  [Z3](https://microsoft.github.io/z3guide/docs/logic/Quantifiers/).

The ETP blueprint is a live formalization companion.  Some of its less-developed
sections visibly contain placeholders or conjectural prose; this report relies
only on theorem statements marked formalized, explicit constructions, the final
project paper, and source code.  Tool timings in the ETP paper were collected on
heterogeneous machines and are treated only as observations, not comparative
benchmarks.  Repository prototype counts are likewise local evidence from the
public development corpus.  No claim here establishes expected performance on
held-back Stage 2 instances, a finite countermodel bound for them, or completeness
of any resource-bounded portfolio.
